Where the entropy is.

Add up the entropy of everything in the observable universe and the stars barely register. Nearly all of it sits in black holes.

Gravity turns the logic of the gas box inside out. A gas left alone spreads. Matter that pulls on itself clumps, and for gravity the clumped state is the one with more arrangements. A black hole is the limit: the most entropy that can fit in a region of space, proportional to the area of its horizon. Collapse the Sun into a black hole and its entropy rises about 1019-fold.

The black hole at the centre of our own galaxy holds about 2 × 1090 k. That is more than all the light left over from the Big Bang, across the entire observable universe.

The last row is the ceiling: the most entropy this region of the universe can ever hold, set by the area of its cosmic horizon. Today's total is about a billionth of a billionth of it. Measured by entropy, almost none of the universe's history has happened yet.

Scaled up

Black holes are where the universe's entropy goes, and they are the last things in it to let go. The emptiest stretch of the descent below is spent waiting for them.
