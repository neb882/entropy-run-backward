The Sun

Start with what we can reach. Sunlight takes eight minutes to get from the Sun to you.

One light-day

Voyager 1 left Earth in 1977. It is the farthest thing humans have ever made. On 18 November 2026, after 49 years of travel, it reached one light-day from home.

The gap

Keep going. The nearest star is 4.2 light-years away. At Voyager's speed the trip takes about 75,000 years. Everything in between is empty.

One dot, one star

Now every dot is one star, and most stars have planets. Scroll, and count how many you can get through.

You can stop. This galaxy has about two hundred billion of them.

Pulling back

Everything you just scrolled past is closing into a single point.

The Milky Way

Our galaxy: 100 to 400 billion stars in a disk about 100,000 light-years across. The Sun takes 230 million years to go around it once and has made about twenty trips.

The Local Group

Andromeda, the nearest large galaxy, is 2.5 million light-years away. The light you would see from it tonight left before our species existed. Three large galaxies and about fifty small ones share ten million light-years.

Laniakea

Our supercluster: about 100,000 galaxies in streams 520 million light-years long. Light takes about as long to cross it as complex animals have existed on Earth.

The cosmic web

Pull back again and superclusters are threads. Galaxies gather along filaments and walls, around voids tens to hundreds of millions of light-years wide.

Everything in view

The observable universe: a sphere 93 billion light-years across, holding somewhere between 200 billion and 2 trillion galaxies. There is more beyond the edge. Nobody knows how much.

200,000,000,000,000,000,000,000

stars, give or take a factor of ten

Counting them at one per second would take 460,000 times the present age of the universe. Most of them have planets. All of it, every dot on every screen you just passed, is running on the same loan as the box of gas at the top of this page.
