These particles pass through one another, which makes the box quick to run and changes nothing about the odds: each particle spends half its time on the left, so all of them are there together one instant in 2N. With 24 you would be here a long while. With 100, the wait is already far longer than the universe has existed.

Add particles and the wait runs away from you. For the 360 in the box at the top of this page it is about 10209 years. Nothing in physics puts a wall anywhere on this ladder. If the far future really is a warm bath that lasts forever, then in about 101050 years the vacuum assembles a single brain, complete with the memories of a life, and in about 1010122 years it has tried every arrangement there is.

That has an uncomfortable consequence. Accidents like that would vastly outnumber ordinary observers, and a fluctuated brain holding your exact memories, including the memory of scrolling down this page, could not tell the difference from inside. Most physicists take this as a sign that something in the argument is wrong. Some have shown how empty space might settle into a state where nothing fluctuates at all. The question is open.

Two other things could change the ending. If protons never decay, dead stars outlast the black holes and slowly turn to iron over about 101500 years. And every date in the descent assumes dark energy holds steady. Since 2024, galaxy surveys have hinted that it may be weakening.
